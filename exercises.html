Workbooks

Ten field and institutional exercises for Seychelles and Western Indian Ocean marine data practitioners. Work through them sequentially or use them as standalone reference tools for the task at hand.

This workbook is in development. Exercises are drafts and have not been reviewed for final publication. Content, field labels, and instructions will change before the platform's public launch.
01
Decision brief

Name the Seychelles decision, responsible institution, statutory or policy instrument, affected communities, spatial extent, and decision date. Record which evidence could change the choice. Separate a technical recommendation from the legal authority to decide.

Fields

DecisionInstitutionInstrumentCommunitiesSpatial extentDecision dateEvidence that could change the choiceTechnical recommendation vs. legal authority
02
Source and rights register

For each layer record product/version, provider, custodian, source URL, access date, licence, citation, permitted redistribution, sensitivity, and update frequency. Classify access as public, registered, controlled or unavailable. A reachable landing page does not establish usable data access.

Fields

Product/versionProviderCustodianSource URLAccess dateLicenceCitationRedistributionSensitivityUpdate frequencyAccess classification
03
Coordinate and datum worksheet

Record source horizontal CRS, axis order, coordinate units, vertical datum, tidal epoch, geoid model, target CRS, transformation method, and software version. State unknowns explicitly. Do not assign a CRS merely to force an overlay to align.

Fields

Source horizontal CRSAxis orderCoordinate unitsVertical datumTidal epochGeoid modelTarget CRSTransformation methodSoftware versionUnknowns
04
Quality and coverage worksheet

Record acquisition dates, cell size or sampling interval, positional and depth uncertainty, quality flags, missing values, and validation evidence. Identify gaps around outer islands. Compare the product resolution with the decision scale. Retain the original data and processing lineage.

Fields

Acquisition datesCell size or sampling intervalPositional uncertaintyDepth uncertaintyQuality flagsMissing valuesValidation evidenceOuter-island gapsResolution vs. decision scaleProcessing lineage
05
Fitness-for-purpose rubric

Use this operational checklist, not a validated HCAF score. Rate each item on a 0 to 3 scale: 0 = undocumented, 1 = documented limitation, 2 = adequate for the stated use, 3 = independently checked for that use. Assess each dimension separately. Do not average away a critical failure. Navigation and engineering require applicable professional standards.

Items to rate

ProvenanceSpatial coverageTemporal coverageReference compatibilityUncertainty documentationLegal reuseReproducibility
06
National stewardship agreement

Name a national custodian, access approver, technical maintainer, and backup owner. Define hosting location, controlled access, retention, incident contact, licence review, update schedule, recurring cost, and exit arrangements. Record the authority to grant access separately from the ability to host files.

Fields

CustodianAccess approverTechnical maintainerBackup ownerHosting locationAccess controlsRetention policyIncident contactLicence review dateUpdate scheduleRecurring costExit arrangementsAccess authority
07
MSP and MPA evidence chain

Link each proposed action to a current official instrument, spatial layer, uncertainty statement, and stakeholder record. Use authoritative Seychelles gazettes and management plans for legal interpretation. Establish a baseline date, numerator, denominator, update frequency, and accountable indicator owner. A global protected-area record is not a legal boundary determination.

Fields

Proposed actionOfficial instrumentSpatial layerUncertainty statementStakeholder recordBaseline dateIndicator numeratorIndicator denominatorUpdate frequencyIndicator owner
08
Seychelles Bank exercise

Compare a regional bathymetric grid, a sea-surface temperature product, and a candidate reef habitat map for a conservation briefing. Document why their scales, depths, and observation dates differ. Produce an evidence note explaining one supported conclusion, one unsupported conclusion, and the local observations needed next.

Output: Evidence note with three sections: (1) supported conclusion, (2) unsupported conclusion, (3) observations needed next.
